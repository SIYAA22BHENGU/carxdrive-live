Car Tests & Performance Data

Numbers on a specification sheet only tell part of the story. carXdrive brings together performance figures, test results and vehicle specifications so drivers can understand how cars compare in the areas that matter most.

What we look at

carXdrive organises vehicle information across acceleration, top speed, braking, rolling performance, engine output, transmission, tyres, weight, dimensions, fuel consumption, safety and technology. The goal is to make complicated vehicle data easier to compare in one place.

Acceleration

Acceleration figures such as 0–100 km/h are useful for comparing how quickly different cars build speed from a standstill. Results can vary because of road surface, tyres, temperature, fuel load, launch technique and testing equipment, so a single number should never be treated as the whole story.

Rolling performance

Rolling acceleration helps show how strongly a car responds once it is already moving. This can be useful when comparing overtaking performance, engine flexibility, gearing and the way different powertrains deliver their output.

Braking

Braking performance depends on more than brake-disc size. Vehicle mass, tyre grip, road surface, brake temperature and electronic systems can all influence stopping distance. carXdrive presents braking information alongside other vehicle specifications so the result has useful context.

Handling, tyres and weight

Power alone does not determine how a car behaves. Kerb weight, weight distribution, tyre size, drivetrain layout and suspension design all affect cornering, stability and driver confidence. Looking at these areas together gives a more complete picture than comparing horsepower alone.

Fuel economy

Official fuel-consumption figures are useful for comparing cars under standardised conditions, while real-world consumption can change significantly with traffic, driving style, temperature, tyre pressure and route. Where figures differ by market or test method, users should check the exact model and specification.

Manufacturer claims and published test data

Vehicle figures may come from manufacturer specifications or from published testing. These are not always directly comparable. Manufacturer claims are normally produced under controlled conditions, while independent tests may use different surfaces, weather, tyres and measurement equipment.

carXdrive therefore treats performance figures as research data rather than guarantees. Differences between sources can be useful because they show how much real-world conditions can affect a car.

Why the exact variant matters

Two cars with the same model name can perform differently because of engine choice, transmission, drivetrain, optional equipment, model year or regional specification. Always check the exact variant before making a direct comparison.

How to use carXdrive test information

Start with the Cars database to find the exact model you are researching. Then use the Compare tool to place two or three vehicles side by side. Look beyond one headline number and compare performance, weight, fuel use, safety and technology together.
